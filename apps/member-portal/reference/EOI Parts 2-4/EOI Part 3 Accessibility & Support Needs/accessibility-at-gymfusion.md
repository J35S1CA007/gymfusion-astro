# Accessibility at GYMFUSION 

In the sport, fitness, and recreation sectors, accessibility is often broader than disability alone.

#### For a business like GYMFUSION, accessibility can reasonably include:

- People with disabilities (physical, intellectual, sensory, neurological)
- Previous injuries (e.g., ACL reconstruction, shoulder instability, back injuries)
- Current injuries or rehabilitation needs
- Chronic health conditions (e.g., arthritis, diabetes, narcolepsy)
- Age-related limitations
- Temporary conditions (e.g., pregnancy, post-surgery recovery)
- Low fitness levels or deconditioning
- Individual mobility, strength, balance, or coordination limitations

The **common theme** is that a participant may require modifications, progressions, regressions, alternative exercises, additional support, or environmental adjustments to participate safely and successfully.

#### For GYMFUSION:

*Rather than* defining accessibility **solely as disability access,** a *better option* is to describe it as:

> *"GYMFUSION is committed to providing an inclusive and accessible training environment. We work with participants of varying abilities, fitness levels, health conditions, disabilities, and injury histories. Where appropriate, coaches can provide modifications or alternative exercises to support safe participation."*

**Or, more specifically:**

> *"Accessibility includes supporting participants who have disabilities, medical conditions, previous or current injuries, mobility restrictions, or other factors that may affect their ability to participate in standard activities. Coaches will work with participants to identify suitable modifications where reasonably practicable."*

#### Important distinction:

> A previous injury **does not automatically** *constitute a disability,* **but** it can create an *accessibility need.*

**For example:** <br> 

| Situation | Disability? | May Need Accessibility Modifications? |
|-----------|-------------|------------------------------------------|
| Previous ACL reconstruction | Not necessarily | Yes |
| Chronic lower back pain | Not necessarily | Yes |
| Autism | Often considered a disability | Yes |
| Temporary wrist injury | No | Yes |
| Narcolepsy | Can be considered a disability under some legislation | Yes |
| Limited shoulder mobility from old injury | No | Yes |

## For forms and policies:

#### Instead of asking only:

> "Do you have a disability?"

#### consider broader wording such as:

> "Do you have any disability, medical condition, injury history, mobility limitation, or other consideration that may require activity modifications, adjustments, or additional support during participation?"

*This approach captures both disability-related and injury-related accessibility requirements and gives coaches the information they need to plan safe, appropriate modifications.*
